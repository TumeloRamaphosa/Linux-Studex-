## 1. Benchmark community: Dads of Marathon

Not a money league operator — a **scheduling + LFG community** model worth copying for “adult life” segments:

| Element | What they do |
|--------|----------------|
| Origin | [Dads of Destiny](https://www.dadsofmarathon.com/) (since 2014) → [Dads of Marathon](https://www.dadsofmarathon.com/) for Bungie’s Marathon |
| Audience | Parents and anyone with irregular play time; open to non-parents |
| Tools | Squad scheduler (timezone-aware), heat maps, forums, Discord (~3k–13k+ reported scale) |
| Pillars | Family → career → gaming (flex over rank grind) |
| Monetization (public) | Community/giveaways/competitions; no obvious paywall on site |

**Stud-Bot angle:** Corporate “dad leagues” and alumni leagues need the same **low-friction matchmaking**, not only brackets. Pair hub games with **scheduled squads** and internal ladders.

## 2. How the gaming economy makes money

### A. Full video game industry (~$180B+ annually)

| Layer | Who | Typical revenue |
|-------|-----|------------------|
| **Publishers / studios** | Tencent, Sony, Microsoft, Nintendo, NetEase, EA, etc. | Game sales, **F2P IAP**, battle passes, DLC |
| **Platforms** | Steam, App Store, Play Store, console stores | **30% (variable) store take**, discovery |
| **Live ops** | Same publishers | Season passes, cosmetics, gacha (where legal) |
| **Hardware** | Sony, Microsoft, Nintendo, PC OEMs | Consoles, GPUs, peripherals |
| **Cloud / infra** | AWS, Azure, etc. | Backend for multiplayer |

### B. Esports & competitive layer (~$2B–$7B depending on definition)

Common **esports revenue stack** (industry analysts, 2024–2025):

| Stream | Share (approx.) | Notes |
|--------|-----------------|-------|
| **Sponsorship** | ~40–60% | Brands buying youth + engagement (energy, auto, fashion, tech) |
| **Advertising** | ~20–30% | Programmatic on Twitch, YouTube, short-form |
| **Media rights** | Fastest growth | Exclusive broadcast deals |
| **Tickets + merch** | ~10–15% | LAN finals, limited drops (e.g. org collabs with apparel houses) |
| **Publisher fees / licensing** | Structural | Official leagues tied to game IP |
| **Betting / adjunct** | Regulated markets only | Mexico/LatAm often cited as growth; strict rules elsewhere |

### C. Scholastic / corporate B2B (your lane)

| Buyer | Pays for | Providers (examples) |
|-------|----------|----------------------|
| **Schools / districts** | Curriculum + COPPA-safe leagues | [NASEF](https://www.nasef.org/), [USAEL](https://www.usacademicesports.com/), PlayVS-style operators, [DAIGON](https://www.daigonesports.com/regions/africa) |
| **Employers / HR** | Engagement, retention, charity | [CEA](https://cea.gg/), [TETO Corporate](https://tetogames.com/corp/en/corporate-games), [Ireland Esports Office Showdown](https://company.irelandesportsleagues.com/about-us) |
| **Sponsors** | Logo, content, recruiting | Same as esports, but smaller CPM, higher “culture” story |

**Stud-Bot monetization paths (no code implied):** SaaS per school/company season, white-label hub, sponsorship packages, licensed game bundles, training + caster packages, merchandise via org not consumer gambling.

## 3. Big brands (who to know)

### Publishers / platforms (revenue leaders)

Public rankings vary by year and whether hardware/store revenue is included. Recurring top names:

- **Tencent** — mobile + stakes in Riot (LoL, Valorant)
- **Sony Interactive Entertainment** — PlayStation + studios
- **Microsoft Gaming** — Xbox, Game Pass, Activision Blizzard

- **EA, Take-Two, Ubisoft** — sports, AAA, live service

Team Liquid, FaZe, T1, G2, Cloud9, Fnatic, etc. — money from sponsors, merch drops, content, not game IP.

### League / infrastructure brands

- **Blizzard / Microsoft** — OWL history, etc.

### Lifestyle / sponsor brands in esports

Red Bull, Monster, apparel (Nike, Puma, Gucci collabs on org merch), telcos, banks (esp. emerging markets).

## 4. Children’s leagues vs corporate leagues

| Dimension | Children / schools | Corporate / workplace |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Goal** | STEM, SEL, attendance, college paths | Retention, cross-team ties, employer brand |
| **Compliance** | COPPA, FERPA, parental consent, age splits | NDAs, brand safety, no gambling hooks |
| **Format** | Clubs → district → national → world (e.g. DAIGON → Malta worlds) | In-house cup → inter-company season |
| **Titles** | Minecraft, Rocket League, LoL (older teens), sports sims | LoL, Valorant, FC, chess, party games |
| **Revenue** | District contracts, grants, sponsor “education” budgets | HR budget, CSR charity (CEA model) |

## 5. Regional snapshot

### Africa

| Operator / program | Focus | Links |
|-------------------|--------|-------|
| **ACGL + Acer Nitro ASL** | SA high schools, multi-title prize pools | [ACGL schools](https://acgl.gg/) |
| **DAIGON** | International schools, badges, world championship route | [DAIGON Africa](https://www.daigonesports.com/regions/africa) |
| **Redstone Royale Africa** | Minecraft Education, multi-country school brackets | [royale.africa](https://royale.africa/) |

**Opportunity:** English-medium international schools + local public tiers; mobile-first audience; partner with existing league backbones instead of reinventing anti-cheat.

### United States

| Operator | Focus |
|----------|--------|
| **NASEF** | Free scholastic clubs, tournaments, curriculum nonprofit |
| **USAEL** | K–12 turnkey: PD, curriculum, tiered competition |
| **CEA** | Corporate charity championships (100+ company teams) |
| **PlayVS** | School league infra (also referenced for Mexico/LATAM) |

**Opportunity:** FERPA/COPPA moat; sell “Stud-Bot scheduler + retro hub” as safe practice layer for elementary/middle.

### China

| Reality | Implication |
|---------|-------------|
| Minors: **1h/day** Fri–Sun holidays only (real-name) | Youth **competitive roster age 18+** for many pro circuits |
| Esports events need **licences / filings** | B2B league product = legal + local partner heavy |
| **Hangzhou** etc. as esports cities | Land-based events + media rights |

**Opportunity:** Scholastic **non-ranked** education esports (Minecraft-style, chess, tetris analogs) vs pro pathways; adult corporate leagues less restricted.

### Russia

| Operator | Focus |
|----------|--------|
| **RESF (ФКС России)** | National school league: Dota 2, Tanks Blitz, Tetris, chess, internet search; age **14+** |
| **Moscow school league (МШКЛ)** | Grades 5–8, 9–11, colleges; 12 disciplines |
| **Sber-backed national cups** | Adult/student tiers |

**Opportunity:** State-shaped school pipeline; integrate “intellectual” disciplines (chess, search) for brand-safe youth positioning.

### Mexico & LATAM

| Fact | Source angle |
|------|----------------|
| **~74M+ gamers**, mobile majority | Brand activations in-game + esports |
| Esports market estimates **~USD 1.3B** (2025, industry reports) | Sponsorship + betting adjacency |
| **FEMES** federation; esports recognized as sport (2019) | Legitimacy for school/corp tenders |
| **NASEF Mexico**, Esports Academy + PlayVS | Scholastic entry |

**Opportunity:** Spanish-first Stud-Bot hub, corporate seasons aligned to Q1/Q3, influencer co-streams.

## 6. Strategic fits for Studex / Stud-Bot

1. **Hub (built):** Retro browser catalog = **on-ramp** and compliance-friendly demos; not the main revenue engine.
2. **League OS:** Brackets, school/company tenancy, era tags (retro vs new), audit logs — align with Agent OS narrative.
3. **Segments:**
   - **Youth:** Partner DAIGON / NASEF-style frameworks in Africa + Mexico; USA through curriculum partners.
   - **Corporate:** Office Showdown / TETO-like managed seasons + charity layer.
   - **Lifestyle:** Dads-of-Marathon-style **schedulers** for adult community retention.
4. **Revenue order (typical):** B2B season fees → sponsorship → premium analytics for schools/HR → merch last.
5. **Avoid on consumer surfaces:** Real-money betting, unlicensed minors in ranked prizing (China especially).
